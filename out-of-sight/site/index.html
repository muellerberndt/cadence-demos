What you are looking at

The scene

A table seen from above. A camera in the corner lights what it sees. The screen and the arm cast shadows, and whatever stands in shadow is out of view: the camera reports nothing of it. While a thing is in view the camera reports its position, a centimetre off on average.

At every moment the brain is asked one question: where is the hand? The teal glow is its answer. The ring around it is the area the brain says holds the hand nine times in ten: it grows while the hand is unseen and closes when the hand is seen again.

Things that stay put

You know there is a bicycle a few metres to your right without looking at it, because it does not move by itself. The brain keeps the cup, the wrench and the box the same way: one record each, drawn as a pin. Move a thing while the camera cannot see it, and the pin stays at the old place. Knock on it, and the ear hears the direction and the distance of the sound: the record moves without sight. A dashed pin came from a sound.

The brain

Cadence is a library for brains that answer by settling: the neurons drive each other until their activity rests, and the answer is read from the resting state. The panel draws every neuron and every synapse of the brain that runs on this page, laid out as a brain seen from the side. Each population sits where the part whose role it plays would sit; hover over one to read what it holds. The lines flash where a lesson changed a synapse. The brain learns only at moments the camera sees the hand, so the flashes stop in the shadow.
