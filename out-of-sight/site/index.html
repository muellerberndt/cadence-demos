The scene

A room seen from above. A body in the room carries a camera, which lights what it sees. The pillar and the arm cast shadows, and whatever stands in shadow, or beside or behind the body, is out of view: the camera reports nothing of it. In view, the camera reports a position about a centimetre off.

The brain holds a map of what is where. A thing enters the map when the camera first sees it. The teal glow is where the map holds the hand, and the light under it the cells that hold it.

The comparison

Two controls are given the same camera reports: the last seen place, and a constant-velocity Kalman filter that carries the hand on along its seen movement. The chart shows each one’s distance from the true hand beside the map’s. The map moves nothing it does not sense. The second stage, learned physics on the map, follows in a later release.

The brain

Cadence is a library for brains that answer by settling: the neurons drive each other until their activity rests, and the answer is read from the resting state. The right half of the page draws every neuron and every synapse of the map’s graph, laid out as a brain seen from the side. Each region sits where the part whose role it plays would sit. Point at a part and it explains itself.
