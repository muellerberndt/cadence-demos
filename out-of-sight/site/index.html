The scene

A room seen from above. A body in the room carries a camera, which lights what it sees. The pillar and the arm cast shadows, and whatever stands in shadow, or beside or behind the body, is out of view: the camera reports nothing of it. In view, the camera reports a position about a centimetre off.

At every moment the brain is asked one question: where is the hand? The teal glow is its answer. The ring around it is the area the brain says holds the hand nine times in ten.

What is where, around you

You know there is an apple behind you and a slice of bread in front of you without looking, because they do not move by themselves. The brain keeps the cup, the apple and the bread the same way: one record each, which holds what the thing is together with where it is. Turn the camera away, and the record stays. The small map shows the same records as the body has them: you at the centre, facing up. A dashed ring came from a sound.

The brain

Cadence is a library for brains that answer by settling: the neurons drive each other until their activity rests, and the answer is read from the resting state. The right half of the page draws every neuron and every synapse of the brain that runs here, laid out as a brain seen from the side. Each population sits where the part whose role it plays would sit. Point at a part and it explains itself.
