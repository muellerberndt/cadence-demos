The scene

A table seen from above. A camera in the corner lights what it sees. The pillar and the arm cast shadows, and whatever stands in shadow is out of view: the camera reports nothing of it. In view, the camera reports a position about a centimetre off.

At every moment the brain is asked one question: where is the hand? The teal glow is its answer. The ring around it is the area the brain says holds the hand nine times in ten.

Things that stay put

You know there is a bicycle a few metres to your right without looking at it, because it does not move by itself. The brain keeps the cup, the wrench and the box the same way: one record each, drawn as a pin. A dashed pin came from a sound.

The brain

Cadence is a library for brains that answer by settling: the neurons drive each other until their activity rests, and the answer is read from the resting state. The right half of the page draws every neuron and every synapse of the brain that runs here, laid out as a brain seen from the side. Each population sits where the part whose role it plays would sit. Point at a part and it explains itself.
