Cadence walkers

Three walkers on three treadmills, the same drive every moment. A step on the other foot than the last one earns a step forward; a repeated foot is a stumble. Nobody tells the walkers what to do: the world pays, that is all. Two of them are Cadence brains born in this page and learning here, from the same founder weights. One carries a copy of its own last command; the other does not, and nothing in what it sees says which foot moved last. The third flips a coin.

Routine: a calm walker answers with the greedy choice of one settle of the whole brain and changes nothing. Aroused: a want (its need unmet) or a surprise (a contradicted forecast) makes it sample, keep eligibility over its own steps and learn from every outcome. The copy is one neuron per motor neuron carrying the one-hot of the last command, read by the association region through a plastic projection; what to do after what it did is learned. Every constant here is a gene of the released library at the reward-rhythm chamber's declared point; the copy's founder value, zero, is the second walker.
